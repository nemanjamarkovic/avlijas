Funeral repatriation services from all of Europe

Losing someone far from home is hard enough without having to work out foreign rules, forms and offices. Many of the families who contact us live in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, the Netherlands, Belgium or the UK, while their parents and grandparents are buried in a village in Serbia or Bosnia. Others are relatives in the Balkans who have just received the news that someone has died abroad.

Whatever the starting point, the task is the same: the deceased has to be released by the authorities of the country of death, the country of burial has to allow the remains in, and the coffin has to travel safely and with dignity. We organise all of it. We are available 24/7, we work with specialised funeral vehicles, and our team speaks German, which helps with hospitals and offices in Germany, Austria and Switzerland. We answer enquiries in English by e-mail and WhatsApp.

Where we bring the deceased

We repatriate to four countries, each with its own entry permit and its own practical details:

  • Serbia: entry permit from the Serbian embassy or consulate (the sprovodnica), delivery to any cemetery in the country.
  • Bosnia and Herzegovina: permit from the BiH mission plus a form for Republika Srpska, the Federation of BiH or Brčko District.
  • Montenegro: permit from the Montenegrin mission, by road through Serbia or along the Croatian coast, or by air.
  • North Macedonia: permit from the Macedonian mission and, by road, a Serbian transit permit.

How it works, step by step

Most repatriations follow the same sequence, although the names of the documents change from country to country:

  1. You call or send a WhatsApp message and tell us where the deceased is and where the burial will be.
  2. We give you the exact price in that first conversation, with no hidden costs.
  3. We contact the hospital, care home or local funeral director and arrange collection and preparation.
  4. We obtain the death certificate, the export permit of the country of death and the entry permit of the country of burial.
  5. The coffin travels by road or air, and we keep you informed until it is handed over at the cemetery or the family home.

What the family needs to do

Very little. We need the name of the deceased, where they died, where they are to be buried and a copy of their passport or identity card. Where an authority requires it, we send you a short authorisation to sign so that we can apply on the family's behalf. Everything else — the Serbian permit (sprovodnica) or the permit of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro or North Macedonia, translations, apostilles and border formalities — is our job. The page on documents for repatriation explains each paper in detail.

By road or by air

From Germany, Austria, Switzerland and most of Central Europe we usually recommend road transport: the funeral vehicle drives from the place of death to the cemetery without any transfer, and it is often just as quick as a flight once cargo booking and airport procedures are counted. From Scandinavia, Spain or other very distant places, air freight to Belgrade, Sarajevo, Podgorica or Skopje can save several days, and we meet the coffin at the airport.

For insurers, assistance companies and funeral directors abroad

If a travel or repatriation insurance policy covers the costs, the insurer or assistance company can contact us directly by e-mail with the case details. Funeral directors, hospitals and social workers in Germany, Austria and Switzerland can also reach us by phone in German. Each case has one contact person, so you always know whom to ask about the status of the documents or the expected arrival time.
